Build a simple third-person 3D grappling traversal game using a capsule-like character, solid platforms and visible anchor rings. The player moves with WASD and aims with the mouse. Holding the right mouse button attaches one rope to the visible anchor under the reticle if it is within 15 m and unobstructed; releasing the button detaches. Show range/validity feedback. Only one rope may be active, and it cannot pass through a blocking wall.

Set rope length from the distance at attachment. Tension prevents exceeding that length while allowing free pendulum motion and slack; do not teleport the player to an anchor or pull along a scripted arc. Releasing retains current velocity. Gravity, platform contacts and limited directional air steering determine the next motion. Allow jumping from a platform. Keep the camera stable and handle obstructions so landings remain readable.

Generate an endless sequence of platform-and-anchor modules from a seed. Randomize the horizontal edge-to-edge gap between each pair of consecutive platforms within a bounded range, rather than repeating a fixed spacing. Choose and document this range to suit the implemented movement and rope physics. Generate compatible anchor positions so every consecutive platform transition is reachable using the existing grapple, swing and release controls: anchors must be visible and within the 15 m attachment limit at an attainable attachment position, the character and rope paths must be unobstructed, and a supported landing area must be attainable under the actual gravity, rope length and steering limits. Anchor distance alone does not prove reachability. Use validated module/gap combinations or reject and resample invalid combinations; do not leave impossible gaps in the course or compensate with teleportation, automatic launches or changed controls. The same seed reproduces the gap and anchor sequence, including after retry; different seeds produce varied spacing. Start with an easy tutorial gap. Color checkpoints and hazards clearly. Landing on a new checkpoint records progress; falling below the course or touching a hazard respawns at the last checkpoint, clears the rope and resets velocity. Measure best forward progress and attempts rather than requiring one timed trick sequence. Allow retrying the same seed, a new seed, pause and restart. Recycle distant modules while keeping the current checkpoint and visible route valid.
