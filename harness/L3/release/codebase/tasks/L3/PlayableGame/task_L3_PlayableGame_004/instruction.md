Build an original, playable third-person 3D stacking game in a restrained geometric style. The player must build the tallest stable tower on one fixed square platform. The game should feel like a complete small game with clean materials, readable silhouettes, simple lighting, a follow camera, clear placement feedback, and a result screen. Do not use a pre-recorded animation or a scripted tower as a substitute for physics.

Start with a square platform 10 m by 10 m, with its top at Z=0. Use a solid collision mesh and a visible boundary so the playable area is clear. At the start of each turn, provide one new rigid piece at a fixed holding plane 4 m above the current highest stable tower point. The holding plane is horizontal and its height remains fixed for that turn; it must always be above the tower so the piece can be released without starting inside existing geometry. While the piece is held, it follows player controls and does not collide with the platform or tower. Show a translucent placement preview and a simple shadow or contact guide, but do not let the preview change the physical result.

Use four geometric piece families: a rectangular bar, a cube, a cylinder and a wedge. The bar may be elongated along any one local axis; the cube must be visibly different from the bar; the cylinder must have a circular cross-section and a flat base; the wedge must have a triangular side profile and a flat supporting face. Choose dimensions within 0.6–3.2 m, keep each piece a closed editable mesh, and give every spawned piece a collision shape that follows its rendered mass. Use physically meaningful mass, friction and restitution values and keep them in the project or README so the evaluator can inspect them. Pieces must not be fused into a single baked tower mesh.

During the holding phase, translate the piece horizontally in X and Y and rotate it around all three local axes. Use clearly documented keyboard and mouse controls; translation and rotation must be continuous and bounded rather than teleporting the piece. Left click releases the piece. After release, remove player control, enable collision and let the engine simulation settle the piece. Do not snap it to a grid, rotate it to a preferred orientation, or move it by animation after release. A released piece must retain its actual rigid-body response, including contacts, friction and tipping.

A turn is stable only after the released piece and all previously placed pieces have remained below 0.05 m/s linear speed and 5 degrees/s angular speed for 2 continuous seconds. Then freeze no geometry, record the new stable height and begin the next turn with a deterministic random piece. The default random seed is 20260922 and it must be changeable from a small settings panel or documented launch option. The same seed and input sequence must produce the same piece-family sequence. Show the current seed and turn number in the HUD.

The run ends when a released piece or any tower piece falls below the platform top, leaves the platform boundary by more than 1 m, or the tower becomes unstable for 5 seconds without satisfying the stability test. Do not end a run merely because a piece touches an edge or because a small contact impulse occurs. When the run ends, show the highest recorded stable top height in metres and a score equal to that height in centimetres rounded down. Include restart, return-to-menu and repeatable rematch actions. The result screen must not leave old rigid bodies, timers or effects in the next run.

Use a stable camera that keeps the active piece and the full upper tower readable; let the camera rise or zoom out as the tower grows. Provide a start menu, controls overlay, current height, best height, score, turn number, stability countdown and game-over result. Add restrained audio or visual feedback for pickup, release, impact, stability and collapse. A local single-player experience is sufficient; network play is not required.

Use a fixed 60 Hz gameplay and physics update independent of render frame rate. The game must remain playable when the tower is short or tall, and the evaluator must be able to replay a fixed sequence of translation, rotation and release inputs. Submit the complete editable UE5 project and Blender source assets using the fixed delivery contract below.

The submitted project must use output/ue5_project/3DBenchGame.uproject as its fixed UE5 project entry. The project root must contain Config/ and Content/; Source/, Plugins/, and SourceAssets/Blender/ are allowed when used. Put a short launch and controls note in output/README.md. Do not submit generated Binaries/, Intermediate/, Saved/, or DerivedDataCache/ directories.
