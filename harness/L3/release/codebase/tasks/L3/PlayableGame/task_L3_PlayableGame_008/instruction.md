Build a small 3D air-hockey game against a simple AI. Use one flat rectangular rink, low solid sidewalls, a goal opening at each short end, two circular strikers and one puck. View the entire rink from a fixed elevated camera. Move the player's striker toward the mouse position projected onto the near half of the rink, with a finite speed cap rather than teleportation. Neither striker may cross the center line or leave its allowed half.

The puck slides on the rink and responds to real striker and wall collisions. Use low drag and a documented restitution value; do not script shots toward goals or pull the puck to a target. Prevent fast motion from tunneling through the striker or wall. A goal occurs only when the whole puck passes through the corresponding goal mouth, not when it touches the wall beside it. Count each goal once, show a short celebration, then reset puck and strikers and give a visible countdown before restarting play.

The AI controls only its own striker and uses observed puck position and velocity to defend or attempt rebounds. Apply the same movement limits as the player. Provide easy and standard modes by changing reaction delay and prediction error, never puck speed, goal size or hidden collision rules. It cannot read future player input.

Offer first-to-seven matches and unlimited practice with scores but no final victory limit. In match mode, the seventh goal opens a result screen with rematch and menu actions. Pause stops physics and the countdown. Reset removes old collision/goal events so rematches cannot award phantom points. Display score and difficulty; create simple audible and visual impact feedback. One arena is sufficient, but rebounds should support varied play.

Use Blender + UE5 to deliver an editable, actually playable 3D game.
