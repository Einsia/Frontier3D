Build a 3D domino-chain playground on a simple bounded tabletop. Offer plain colored domino slabs, a ball, a small ramp and a bell target. Use an orbit camera and let players place, rotate, duplicate and delete parts. Provide an optional line/arc placement brush with adjustable spacing so long chains are pleasant to create. All parts are simple solid meshes; dominoes need no dot textures. Start with a short chain that works and allow an empty table.

Separate Build and Simulate. In Simulate, the player may give one chosen domino a single small push, or release the ball once; subsequent movement must arise from rigid-body gravity and contact. Do not schedule falls or trigger distant pieces merely by chain order. A deliberately enlarged gap must be able to break the chain. Static ramps remain fixed; dominoes and the ball remain physical.

Show how many dominoes have fallen and which bell targets were struck. A domino counts as fallen when its up axis tilts more than 60 degrees from its initial upright axis, counted at most once per trial. Each target chimes and counts once when physically contacted by a moving play piece. Keep simulation running until the player stops it; there is no mandatory time limit or final level. Reset restores every part's pre-run transform and clears velocities, contacts, sound triggers and counts. Save/load the pre-run design, not a partially collapsed result, and support repeated experiments.
